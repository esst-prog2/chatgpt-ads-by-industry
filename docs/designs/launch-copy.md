## Problem Statement

The ChatGPT Ads Industry Dashboard works, but only its builder uses it. The goal is to get the first 2-3 PPC managers at the agency to open it for a real client media plan. The user asked for "the content of my program's promo page". This session reframed that as a launch page that recruits first users, because no colleague has asked for these numbers yet.

## Demand Evidence

None yet. Asked for the strongest evidence that a colleague wants this, the user answered: "No direct ask yet". The expected demand rests on an unverified assumption: as clients move budget to ChatGPT Ads, PPC managers will need platform-specific numbers when planning. Evidence that would change this: one dated request from a colleague for ChatGPT Ads CPC/CTR figures.

## Status Quo

PPC managers handle ChatGPT Ads with "test budget, then adjust": they launch a small test budget without platform-specific estimates and correct it after 1-2 weeks. The cost is one or two weeks of client spend at unknown efficiency, plus a "we're still learning" conversation with the client. This is a sane default and the page must not claim to replace it.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **Target user:** PPC managers who create media plans for clients (user's words: "PPC Managers who creates media plans for clients"). First concrete case: **PPC Manager A**, with an upcoming ChatGPT Ads plan for a **Professional Services** client, who would open the dashboard while setting the test budget. Labels only, no real names.
- **Narrowest wedge:** "see what real campaigns pay in your client's industry before you size the first test budget". The wedge is read-only and needs no setup beyond an invite.
- **Data reality for that case:** the account mapping lists 2 real Professional Services accounts. That's below the Rule of 3 (`app/Services/DashboardService.php:44`), so that industry's view still blends in synthetic data, and the planned benchmark (draft benchmark design in the local gstack store) would show "No benchmark yet".

## Constraints

- The app is internal: everything except `/login` and the Google OAuth routes requires sign-in (`routes/web.php`). There is no public site.
- CLAUDE.md privacy rules: no real client, partner or company names, and no personal data, in tracked files.
- DESIGN_AGENT.md: tables and box plots are not redesigned. Reader-facing copy changes only on the login page and in the invitation email. Supporting code changes touch the admin invite form (`resources/views/admin/index.blade.php`), `UserAccessController::store` and `UserInvited`, plus the logo `alt` fix in `login.blade.php` and `dashboard.blade.php`.
- What the dashboard actually shows: only the **account** rows in an industry's drill-down carry a Real/Synthetic tag; each account's campaigns are listed under it, in that account's own currency. The box plot and "Industry totals" are untagged and blend synthetic data for any industry below the Rule of 3. The copy may only point readers at what's tagged.
- The user chose to keep the session private: no web research.

## Premises

All six agreed by the user:

1. The page's job is to **recruit the first 2-3 PPC managers** with an upcoming ChatGPT Ads plan, not to promote broadly. Success = a colleague opens it for a real client plan.
2. **Lead with the job, not the features.** The tool sits alongside the test budget and never claims to replace it.
3. **Promise only what's live.** The page tells readers only rows marked "Real" are real campaigns. The benchmark appears as "coming", with what unlocks it.
4. **No client names and no single-client figures** on the page; example numbers are labelled illustrative.
5. **It lives where invited colleagues already land:** the login page plus the invitation email. No new public website.
6. **English**, matching the app's UI.

## Recommended Approach

Because `GoogleAuthController` returns `redirect()->intended('/')` (`app/Http/Controllers/Auth/GoogleAuthController.php:91`), a deep link survives the sign-in step.

### Copy: login page (`resources/views/auth/login.blade.php`)

Order: job, how, honesty, action.

- **Eyebrow:** Internal tool · ChatGPT Ads beta
- **Headline:** Planning ChatGPT Ads for a client?
- **Subhead:** See what real campaigns on the platform pay, by industry, before you set the test budget.
- **Steps** (each points at something the drill-down actually shows):
  1. **Pick your client's industry.** The dashboard opens on the last 28 days and lists that industry's accounts and campaigns.
  2. **Look only at campaigns under accounts marked Real.** Those are live ChatGPT Ads campaigns from accounts we manage. Accounts marked Synthetic fill gaps until an industry has 3 real accounts; the chart and industry totals include them, so don't budget on those.
  3. **Note the CPC and CTR of those real campaigns before you size the first test budget,** then compare your test against them after week one. CPCs are in each account's own currency; the page footer shows the HUF rate.
- **Coming next box:** Coming next: a one-line industry benchmark you can paste into a plan, shown once an industry has enough real data.
- **Button:** Sign in with Google (existing route, unchanged)
- **Fine print:** No client names are shown. Access is by invitation: ask an admin.
  - v1 shows **no account or industry counts** on the login page. That avoids exposing the beta's size on an unauthenticated page and needs no new counting code.

### Copy: invitation email (`app/Notifications/UserInvited.php`)

- **Subject:** Planning ChatGPT Ads for a client? See what real campaigns pay
- **Greeting:** Hi,
- **Line 1:** You now have **{role}** access to the ChatGPT Ads Industry Dashboard. It shows what live ChatGPT Ads campaigns pay, by industry, from accounts we manage.
- **Line 2:** Use it before you set a client's first test budget: open their industry and note the CPC and CTR of the campaigns under accounts marked **Real**. CPCs are in each account's own currency; the dashboard footer shows the HUF rate.
- **Button:** `Open {industry} on the dashboard` when the admin picked a client industry; otherwise `Open the dashboard`. The link is built with `url()->query('/', ['industry' => $industry, 'metric' => 'cpc'])` (not `url('/', [...])`, which would add path segments instead of a query string), so values are URL-encoded. Several industry names contain `&`, and a hand-concatenated string would break them.
- **Line 3:** Coming soon: a one-line benchmark you can paste straight into a media plan.
- **Closing (unchanged):** If you weren't expecting this, you can ignore this email.
- `{role}` stays the final resolved role, as today.

### Small code changes the copy needs

1. **Admin invite form:** an optional "Client industry" select. It lists **only industries that currently have at least one Real-labelled account** (the industries `DashboardService` would show with real data), so the button never lands on an industry with no view. Blank keeps today's behavior.
2. **`UserAccessController::store`:** validate the field server-side: `nullable` and `in:` that same industry list. Anything else is a validation error, never silently dropped. Pass the validated value to `UserInvited`.
3. **`UserInvited`:** accept an optional industry and build the link as described above. `DashboardController` validates `from`/`to`/`metric` but not `industry` (an unknown industry is ignored and shows the dashboard without a drill-down). That's why validity is enforced at invite time, not at click time.
4. **Login view:** new copy only.

## Reviewer Concerns

Stop: PASS

No blocking findings remain. The 5 open minor finding(s) below are recorded for the user, not fixed.

### R2-1 — feasibility (minor)

**Problem**

> 'Laravel's URL helper from a query array' does not name a call, and the obvious reading is wrong here. The dashboard route `/` has no name (routes/web.php line 19), so route() can't be used, and url('/', [...]) / URL::to() append extra parameters as path segments, not a query string. That would produce something like /Professional%20Services/cpc, not ?industry=...&metric=cpc. The '&' feature test in Success Criteria would catch this, which is why it is minor.

**Remedy**

> Name the exact call: url()->query('/', ['industry' => $industry, 'metric' => 'cpc']) (available on the Laravel ^13 framework in composer.json), or url('/').'?'.http_build_query([...]) as dashboard.blade.php line 194 already does.

### R2-2 — clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> The design doesn't say how the admin controller computes this list. DashboardService::activeRealAccountCountsByIndustry() is private (DashboardService.php line 117) and maps account ids with IndustryMapping. UserAccessController::unifiedAccounts() derives database accounts' industry from AdsAccount->industry instead. A second, hand-written count in the admin controller could drift from what the dashboard actually drills into. Both the select and the server-side in: rule must use one source.

**Remedy**

> State that one shared public method (e.g. DashboardService::industriesWithRealAccounts()) feeds both the select options and the Rule::in() check in store(), and note that this is new code, unlike the login page.

### R2-3 — consistency (minor)

**Problem**

> 'Never' overclaims. The list is checked at invite time, but UserInvited is queued (ShouldQueue) and the email may be clicked days later. If the industry's only Real account has been removed or deactivated by then, DashboardService ignores the industry (line 87) and the recipient sees the dashboard with no drill-down. The design states that fallback in code change 3, so the behavior is known, but the 'never' wording contradicts it.

**Remedy**

> Reword to 'at the time of the invite', and keep the stated fallback (dashboard without a drill-down) as the accepted behavior for stale links.

### R2-4 — clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> Persisting R1-11. Constraints now acknowledge that campaigns show in each account's own currency (dashboard.blade.php lines 219-220, 229-230). But login step 3 and email Line 2 still ask readers to note campaign CPCs without saying that real accounts in one industry can be in different currencies, and the client's budget currency may differ too.

**Remedy**

> Add a few words, e.g. 'CPCs are in each account's own currency; the page footer shows the HUF rate.'

### R2-5 — feasibility (minor)

**Problem**

> Persisting R1-12. Open Question 2 still says 'at most one industry could show a benchmark today'. Inputs from the mapping CSV: Education & Careers 2 real, Professional Services 2, and Health, Travel & Hospitality and Software & Technology 1 each. Against a 3-real-account threshold, the count of industries at 3 or more is 0. Reaching 1 needs database-registered accounts, which the document doesn't cite.

**Remedy**

> State the inputs behind 'at most one', or say 'none yet from the .env accounts alone'.

**R1-12 → R2-5 (persisting)**
